Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) vision-language models spend a fixed number of experts K on every token, although questions differ in how much computation they need. We propose DecisionMoE-K, a PPO policy that chooses the number of active experts per question and per generation step under a token-weighted FLOPs budget, on frozen models and without changing the gate; we also study whether TOPSIS multi-criteria expert ranking adds anything. Because a learned router is easy to over-credit, we introduce a matched-cost evaluation protocol: policies are compared at equal token-weighted FLOPs with paired bootstrap against the upper convex hull of all uniform Top-K and static asymmetric ($K_{\mathrm{prefill}}, K_{\mathrm{decode}}$) rules, and the cost term is audited (a step-averaged FLOPs reward is gamed: reported 0.33, true 0.99).
On DeepSeek-VL2-Tiny (64 routed experts, top-6) and three brief-chain-of-thought multiple-choice tasks, DecisionMoE-K ($\lambda$=0.4, 15 seeds) is never significantly below the best static reference (all CIs include zero or are positive), and where the Top-K accuracy curve has a cliff it is significantly above it: on ScienceQA-IMG it keeps +1 to +8 points over the hull (four of five seeds with CIs excluding zero) by choosing K per question, with 14--22\% fewer FLOPs than Top-3 at 1--4 points lower accuracy. The ScienceQA gain replicates on an independent 597-question slice (all seven policies above the hull, six of seven intervals excluding zero). Measured wall-clock savings are much smaller than the FLOPs savings. On AI2D a static asymmetric rule (small K in prefill, larger in decode) explains the whole gain, and on A-OKVQA the policy rediscovers Top-3 or Top-2. Policies are task-specific (AI2D policies fail on ScienceQA-IMG) and unstable at stronger cost pressure. TOPSIS re-ranking adds no accuracy even with an oracle domain label, and on a sparsely upcycled model (MoE-LLaVA) expert choice does not matter at all. We conclude that learned adaptive K is a safe, sometimes beneficial controller, but that most apparent gains disappear against a proper static reference.
